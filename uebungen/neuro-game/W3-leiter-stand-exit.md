# W3 · Leiter-Stand-Exit

*Coordination ladder into named stance*

**Warm-up** · **8 min** · **All ages** · **Proprioceptive + Motor Chaining**

---

## Neuro-Target

Proprioceptive sequencing + motor-program chaining. The ladder demands conscious foot placement; the exit stance forces immediate recomposure — bridging fine footwork to TKD structural positions.

## Taekwondo Technique

Exit stances: Ap Seogi · Juchum Seogi · Dwit Seogi. Hold 2 seconds. Coach calls stance at last square — no anticipation allowed.

✓ **Correct stance — called at last moment, no pre-loading**

## TKD Link

Footwork precision · stance transition under movement · sparring footwork patterns

## Setup

1–2 coordination ladders (or tape marks on floor, 40cm squares). Space: 5m run-up.

## How to Play

1. Athletes run the ladder pattern (in-in-out, single-foot, crossover)
2. As athlete exits the last square, coach calls a stance name
3. Athlete lands into that stance — holds 2s — returns to start
4. Cue is called late (when athlete is at 3rd-to-last square) to prevent prep

## Variations

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| Slow 1-foot pattern · stance cue given early · only 2 stances | Crossover patterns · add block on exit · adults: exit into Dollyo Chagi chamber |

## Why It Works Neurologically

The ladder occupies conscious proprioceptive bandwidth — forcing automatic processing of the stance exit through basal ganglia rather than prefrontal cortex. This is the same channel that needs to fire in sparring: footwork becomes automatic while attention stays on the opponent.
