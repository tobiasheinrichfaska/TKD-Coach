# W1 · Farben-Chagi

*Color kick — visual cue to kick selection*

**Warm-up** · **6–8 min** · **All ages** · **Visual-Motor**

---

## Neuro-Target

Visual processing → motor selection → execution. Forces the nervous system to select and fire a full motor program under uncertainty — exactly what happens when reading an opponent's opening.

## Taekwondo Technique

Ap Chagi · Dollyo Chagi · Yop Chagi — all executed with full chamber and controlled retraction. The kick is unchanged; only the trigger is neural.

✓ **Full technique — form preserved**

## TKD Link

Reaction kicks · sparring read-and-respond · cue-based combination entry

## Setup

3–4 colored cones or A4 paper sheets (red, blue, yellow). Pads optional. Min. 3m working space per person.

## How to Play

1. Assign: Red = Ap Chagi · Blue = Dollyo Chagi · Yellow = Yop Chagi
2. Athletes stand in fighting stance, weight neutral, no anticipation
3. Coach lifts or calls a color — athletes execute the matching kick immediately
4. Hold retraction 1 second, return to stance. Repeat on next cue.
5. Vary cadence: quick bursts → longer pauses → quick bursts (unpredictability is the stimulus)

## Variations

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| 2 colors only · slow cadence · same leg throughout | Opposite-color rule (red = do blue) · two colors = combo · alternating legs |

> **Large group:** pairs — one partner calls color, other kicks. Rotate every 90s.

## Log Metric

Count reaction errors per 10 cues (wrong kick or late response). Note per person or as group average.

## Why It Works Neurologically

Decoupling the visual decision from the stored motor program forces active pattern-matching in the prefrontal cortex before motor cortex fires — building the same neural pathway used in real sparring reads. The opposite-color variant loads the inhibitory system, training the athlete to suppress the habitual response.
