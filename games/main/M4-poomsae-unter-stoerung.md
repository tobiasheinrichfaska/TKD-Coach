# M4 · Poomsae unter Störung

*Motor-program persistence under visual distraction*

**Main** · **18 min** · **All ages** · **Attentional Filtering**

---

## Neuro-Target

Attentional filtering + motor-program persistence. Training the nervous system to maintain a stored motor sequence while the visual system is being deliberately overloaded. Direct transfer to grading anxiety and sparring distractors.

## Taekwondo Technique

Full Poomsae — unmodified, complete sequence at the athlete's level. No technique is removed or simplified. The distractor is external, not built into the technique.

✓ **Full Poomsae — form preserved, sequence maintained**

## TKD Link

Poomsae under grading pressure · opponent-tracking in sparring · sequence memory under competition stress

## Setup

No equipment needed. Partner stands 1m to side of performer. Coach observes.

## How to Play

1. Performer runs their Poomsae — baseline, clean run first
2. Partner stands in peripheral vision and performs slow random hand movements
3. Performer must ignore distractor and complete Poomsae with no sequence errors
4. Coach counts sequence errors (wrong move, missed step, pause before next move)
5. Debrief: where did the sequence stall? That's the weakest node in the stored program.

## Variations

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| Distractor behind performer (not in field of view) · no movement, just presence | Distractor moves into path · verbal counting distractors · time pressure ("finish before I count to 30") |

> **Group format:** 2 performers run simultaneously with 1 distractor each. Observer group counts errors. Adds social pressure — valid neuro-stressor for grading prep.

## Log Metric

Sequence errors vs. baseline clean run. Track "distractors present: X errors" vs. "baseline: Y errors" to quantify attention capacity.

## Why It Works Neurologically

The visual system and the prefrontal "program manager" must compete for resources. Under distraction, poorly consolidated sequences fragment first — revealing which moves are genuinely automatic (cerebellar) and which still require active attention (prefrontal). Repeated exposure under distraction accelerates the consolidation of the sequence into long-term motor memory.
