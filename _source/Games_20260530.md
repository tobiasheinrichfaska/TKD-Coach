# Neuroathletik × Taekwondo — Coach Reference v1.1
*Breitensport · 10 ready-to-use games · warm-up / main / cool-down · kids, youth & adults*

> **Core rule:** The neuro-stimulus is built *into* real Taekwondo movement — not added alongside it. Technique is never compromised for the neuro-element. If a non-TKD movement is required, it is flagged explicitly.

---

## 🔥 Warm-Up
*Activate nervous system · establish visual-motor coupling · prime stances and footwork*

---

### W1 · Farben-Chagi
*Color kick — visual cue to kick selection*

`Warm-up` `6–8 min` `All ages` `Visual-Motor`

**Neuro-Target**
Visual processing → motor selection → execution. Forces the nervous system to select and fire a full motor program under uncertainty — exactly what happens when reading an opponent's opening.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Ap Chagi · Dollyo Chagi · Yop Chagi — all executed with full chamber and controlled retraction. The kick is unchanged; only the trigger is neural.
`✓ Full technique — form preserved`

**TKD Link**
Reaction kicks · sparring read-and-respond · cue-based combination entry

**Setup**
3–4 colored cones or A4 paper sheets (red, blue, yellow). Pads optional. Min. 3m working space per person.

**How to Play**
1. Assign: Red = Ap Chagi · Blue = Dollyo Chagi · Yellow = Yop Chagi
2. Athletes stand in fighting stance, weight neutral, no anticipation
3. Coach lifts or calls a color — athletes execute the matching kick immediately
4. Hold retraction 1 second, return to stance. Repeat on next cue.
5. Vary cadence: quick bursts → longer pauses → quick bursts (unpredictability is the stimulus)

**Variations**

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| 2 colors only · slow cadence · same leg throughout | Opposite-color rule (red = do blue) · two colors = combo · alternating legs |

> Large group: pairs — one partner calls color, other kicks. Rotate every 90s.

**Why It Works Neurologically**
Decoupling the visual decision from the stored motor program forces active pattern-matching in the prefrontal cortex before motor cortex fires — building the same neural pathway used in real sparring reads. The opposite-color variant loads the inhibitory system, training the athlete to suppress the habitual response.

**Log Metric**
Count reaction errors per 10 cues (wrong kick or late response). Note per person or as group average.

### W2 · Spiegel-Stand
*Mirror stance — visual-proprioceptive mirroring*

`Warm-up` `5 min` `All ages` `Visual + Proprioceptive`

**Neuro-Target**
Visual + proprioceptive integration. Mirroring suppresses prediction and forces continuous sensory processing — the anti-habit stimulus. Also trains anticipation reading without acting on it prematurely.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Ap Seogi · Juchum Seogi · step-switches · Makki blocks (Arae, Momtong, Olgul). Form policed: call out any stance collapse for speed.
`✓ Stances and blocks — form preserved`

**TKD Link**
Sparring distance management · opponent-reading · Poomsae stance precision under movement

**How to Play**
1. Pairs face each other, 1.5m apart, both in Ap Seogi
2. Leader changes stances and adds blocks at will — no announcement
3. Partner mirrors instantly (mirror image, not copy)
4. Coach calls "switch" every 30–45s — roles swap
5. Remind athletes: form first, speed second

**Variations**

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| Slow tempo · only 2 stance options · same height throughout | Add direction steps · "freeze" cue (hold 3s, coach checks form) · add kicks |

**Why It Works Neurologically**
Continuous visual input drives proprioceptive adjustment in real-time — the mirror neuron system is activated alongside genuine sensorimotor recalibration. The freeze cue forces the cerebellum to lock in positional accuracy, which transfers directly to Poomsae hold positions.

---

### W3 · Leiter-Stand-Exit
*Coordination ladder into named stance*

`Warm-up` `8 min` `All ages` `Proprioceptive + Motor Chaining`

**Neuro-Target**
Proprioceptive sequencing + motor-program chaining. The ladder demands conscious foot placement; the exit stance forces immediate recomposure — bridging fine footwork to TKD structural positions.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Exit stances: Ap Seogi · Juchum Seogi · Dwit Seogi. Hold 2 seconds. Coach calls stance at last square — no anticipation allowed.
`✓ Correct stance — called at last moment, no pre-loading`

**TKD Link**
Footwork precision · stance transition under movement · sparring footwork patterns

**Setup**
1–2 coordination ladders (or tape marks on floor, 40cm squares). Space: 5m run-up.

**How to Play**
1. Athletes run the ladder pattern (in-in-out, single-foot, crossover)
2. As athlete exits the last square, coach calls a stance name
3. Athlete lands into that stance — holds 2s — returns to start
4. Cue is called late (when athlete is at 3rd-to-last square) to prevent prep

**Variations**

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| Slow 1-foot pattern · stance cue given early · only 2 stances | Crossover patterns · add block on exit · adults: exit into Dollyo Chagi chamber |

**Why It Works Neurologically**
The ladder occupies conscious proprioceptive bandwidth — forcing automatic processing of the stance exit through basal ganglia rather than prefrontal cortex. This is the same channel that needs to fire in sparring: footwork becomes automatic while attention stays on the opponent.

## ⚡ Main Block
*Technique under neuro-load · working memory + vestibular + proprioceptive challenge*

---

### M1 · Zahlen-Kombi
*Working memory + kick combination sequencing*

`Main` `10–12 min` `All ages` `Working Memory + Visual`

**Neuro-Target**
Working memory → sequenced motor output. Holding a number sequence in working memory while executing full-form techniques — exactly the neural demand of a multi-kick combination in sparring.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Full kick and strike techniques: 1 = jab · 2 = Dollyo Chagi · 3 = back kick · 4 = Yop Chagi · 5 = Olgul Makki. All techniques executed with full form, full chamber, correct retraction.
`✓ Full kick/strike technique — form never reduced`

**TKD Link**
Combination sparring · sequence memory · pad work · reaction combinations

**Setup**
Pads (1 per pair). Optional: numbered cards coach holds up. Each pair: 1 kicker + 1 pad holder.

**How to Play**
1. Establish number map (write on board or verbal at start)
2. Coach calls a 2–3 number sequence: "2 – 1 – 3"
3. Kicker executes full sequence on pads, in order
4. Pad holder gives resistance on kicks, no visual cues
5. Rotate kicker/holder every 90s. Increase sequence length as session progresses.

**Variations**

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| 2 numbers max · visual card cue · repeat sequence aloud before executing | 4–5 numbers · reverse order · add distraction noise · no repeat allowed |

> Log: correct sequence completions / attempts per pair. Group race: who completes 5 clean combos first?

**Why It Works Neurologically**
Prefrontal working memory must hold and order the sequence while the motor system executes each full movement. This dual-demand training directly mirrors sparring cognition: planning the next move while executing the current one. Longer sequences overload working memory capacity, forcing faster automatization of individual techniques.

### M2 · Vestibular Dollyo
*Post-rotation balance → full kick execution*

`Main` `12 min` `Youth + Adults` `Vestibular Recalibration`

**Neuro-Target**
Vestibular recalibration: the system must re-establish spatial orientation and stable chamber immediately after rotation — training the reflex that holds spinning-kick landings and lateral cut recoveries.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Full Dollyo Chagi — chamber, extension, snap retraction, controlled landing. The kick is never modified. The pre-kick rotation is the vestibular stimulus.
`✓ Dollyo Chagi — full technique preserved`

> ⚠ The rotation (1–2 spins) is deliberate non-TKD stimulus — justified because no TKD technique generates the same vestibular perturbation. Kick remains authentic. Flag this to athletes.

**TKD Link**
Spinning kick stability · lateral direction change recovery · balance under spatial disorientation

**Setup**
Kick shields at 2–3 stations. Pairs (shield holder + kicker). Open floor 3m per station.

**How to Play**
1. Kicker stands facing shield, arms out, spotting a point on wall
2. Execute 1 full controlled rotation (spotting on wall)
3. On landing: immediate Dollyo Chagi to shield — no pause, no wobble allowed
4. Hold the landing 2s before lowering foot
5. Rotate after 5 reps. Coach watches for stable hips on kick, not just balance.

**Variations**

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| No rotation — close eyes 3s before kick · one-foot stand before kick | 2 rotations · eyes-closed segment · coach calls target height (low/mid/high) |

**Log Metric**
Per set of 5: stable landing / stumble / foot down. Track as S/T/F ratio. "Better" = more S, fewer T and F over weeks.

**Why It Works Neurologically**
Rotation creates endolymph displacement in the semicircular canals, generating a vestibular signal inconsistent with visual input. The nervous system must rapidly recalibrate to execute a controlled motor act. Repeated exposure trains the cerebellum to filter vestibular noise faster — the same mechanism that makes experienced spinning-kick athletes land cleanly.

### M3 · Einbein-Poomsae
*Single-leg Poomsae — proprioceptive overload*

`Main` `12 min` `All ages` `Proprioceptive + Vestibular`

**Neuro-Target**
Single-leg postural control under dual cognitive load: executing a Poomsae arm sequence while the proprioceptive system manages a dynamic single-leg stance. The two tasks compete for cerebellar resources — exactly the condition of executing technique under pressure.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Poomsae arm segments (blocks and strikes) from the athlete's current Poomsae — full correct form, not simplified. Standing on one leg changes the proprioceptive demand, not the arm technique.
`✓ Poomsae arm technique — form preserved and actively trained`

**TKD Link**
Poomsae precision under pressure · kick chamber balance · single-leg stability for all kicks

**Setup**
Yoga mats or similar (for safety). No other equipment. Can run individually or in lines.

**How to Play**
1. Athletes stand on one leg (non-dominant first), free leg in chamber position
2. Execute Poomsae arm sequence (3–5 moves) in correct timing and form
3. Coach counts errors in arm technique AND balance breaks
4. Switch legs after each sequence
5. Progress: eyes closed when stable with eyes open (add slowly)

**Variations**

| ↓ Kids/Beginners | ↑ Advanced |
|---|---|
| Wall touch allowed for first 2 attempts · shorter 2-move sequence · dominant leg only first | Eyes closed · partner creates peripheral visual distraction · unstable surface (foam pad) |

**Log Metric**
Hold time (seconds) before foot touches down — this is the primary retest metric. Note separately: arm technique errors vs. balance breaks.

**Why It Works Neurologically**
The cerebellum handles both proprioceptive balance regulation and motor sequencing. Loading both simultaneously forces more efficient neural processing — the system learns to run the arm program with less active attention, freeing cognitive bandwidth. Eyes-closed removes visual compensation, pushing the vestibular and proprioceptive systems to do more work independently.

### M4 · Poomsae unter Störung
*Motor-program persistence under visual distraction*

`Main` `18 min` `All ages` `Attentional Filtering`

**Neuro-Target**
Attentional filtering + motor-program persistence. Training the nervous system to maintain a stored motor sequence while the visual system is being deliberately overloaded. Direct transfer to grading anxiety and sparring distractors.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Full Poomsae — unmodified, complete sequence at the athlete's level. No technique is removed or simplified. The distractor is external, not built into the technique.
`✓ Full Poomsae — form preserved, sequence maintained`

**TKD Link**
Poomsae under grading pressure · opponent-tracking in sparring · sequence memory under competition stress

**Setup**
No equipment needed. Partner stands 1m to side of performer. Coach observes.

**How to Play**
1. Performer runs their Poomsae — baseline, clean run first
2. Partner stands in peripheral vision and performs slow random hand movements
3. Performer must ignore distractor and complete Poomsae with no sequence errors
4. Coach counts sequence errors (wrong move, missed step, pause before next move)
5. Debrief: where did the sequence stall? That's the weakest node in the stored program.

**Variations**

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| Distractor behind performer (not in field of view) · no movement, just presence | Distractor moves into path · verbal counting distractors · time pressure ("finish before I count to 30") |

> Group format: 2 performers run simultaneously with 1 distractor each. Observer group counts errors. Adds social pressure — valid neuro-stressor for grading prep.

**Why It Works Neurologically**
The visual system and the prefrontal "program manager" must compete for resources. Under distraction, poorly consolidated sequences fragment first — revealing which moves are genuinely automatic (cerebellar) and which still require active attention (prefrontal). Repeated exposure under distraction accelerates the consolidation of the sequence into long-term motor memory.

### M5 · Reaktions-Zahl-Kreis
*Auditory cue → block selection — group re-engagement tool*

`Main` `8 min` `All ages` `Auditory Reaction + Attention`

**Neuro-Target**
Auditory cue processing → block selection → correct motor execution. Trains the auditory-motor pathway independently of visual input. Also a powerful group attention-reset tool.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Makki blocks: 1 = Arae Makki · 2 = Momtong Makki · 3 = Olgul Makki. Full hip rotation, correct arm path, no shortcutting for speed.
`✓ Makki blocks — form preserved and emphasized`

**TKD Link**
Block selection in sparring · combination blocking · reaction to attack patterns

**How to Play**
1. Athletes form a circle, fighting stance, weight centered
2. Coach calls a number (1, 2, or 3) — all execute the matching block simultaneously
3. Wrong block or late = small penalty (5 pushups or 1 round sit-out)
4. Increase call speed progressively. Mix in false starts (long pause → sudden call).

**Variations**

| ↓ Easier | ↑ Harder |
|---|---|
| 2 numbers only · slow cadence · visual card alongside number | 4 options · call in Korean TKD terms · elimination round |

**Why It Works Neurologically**
Isolating auditory input as the sole trigger trains an independent sensorimotor pathway. Many TKD athletes are visually dominant — building an equally fast auditory-motor reflex broadens the neural options available in sparring. The social/penalty element adds mild stress, which is a valid neuro-stressor that mimics competition arousal states.

### M6 · Vestibularer Gangpfad
*Gaze-fixed directional walk — vestibular + visual-spatial*

`Main` `10 min` `All ages` `Vestibular + Gaze Stability`

**Neuro-Target**
Vestibulo-ocular reflex (VOR) + gaze stabilization during multidirectional movement. Trains the visual-vestibular system to maintain target fixation while moving — directly applicable to opponent-tracking during sparring footwork.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Exits into TKD stances or a single kick (Ap Chagi, coach-called) at end of each circuit pass. The walk itself is the vestibular stimulus — not a TKD movement.

> ⚠ The walking circuit is non-TKD — no equivalent TKD movement generates this multi-directional vestibular + gaze stimulus. Justified specifically for sparring lateral-cut eye-contact maintenance. Add a TKD exit action to each pass.

**Protocol**
1. Fix gaze on a single point on the wall (eye height)
2. Walk backward 6m → sideways 6m (peripheral marker) → forward 6m → sideways 6m
3. Complete 3 passes, then 3 passes with opposite lateral direction
4. On completing each full pass: execute coach-called stance or kick immediately
5. Partner watches: eyes must stay on fixation point throughout. Gaze breaks = the metric.

**Age-Specific Notes**

| Kids 8–13 | Adults |
|---|---|
| Expect laughter turns 1–2, improvement turns 3–6. Some dizziness — normalize it. Frame as "spy walk" or gaze challenge. | Will mask difficulty. Need partner accountability for gaze breaks. Frame as elite performance protocol, not balance game. Adults benefit more from turns 4–6 (lateral reversal = sparring cut simulation). Can sustain 8–10 cycles before fatigue — reduce rest between turns to increase density. |

**Progression variant (adults/advanced)**
Add a cognitive dual-task: count backwards in 3s aloud during the walk. Occupies prefrontal resources, prevents deliberate balance correction, forces the vestibular reflex to carry full load. Use only when gaze stability at 6 cycles is already solid.

**Why It Works Neurologically**
The VOR keeps visual targets stable during head/body movement by generating compensatory eye movements. Multi-directional walking while maintaining a fixed gaze point forces rapid, continuous VOR adjustment — the same reflex needed to track an opponent during rapid lateral sparring footwork.

## ❄️ Cool-Down
*Parasympathetic activation · proprioceptive consolidation · session metric capture*

---

### C1 · Balance-Hold Challenge
*Single-leg timed hold — primary progress metric*

`Cool-Down` `8 min` `All ages` `Proprioceptive Baseline`

**Neuro-Target**
Proprioceptive accuracy and vestibular static balance — quantified as a timed hold. This is the primary re-test metric for the whole program.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Single-leg stance in kick-chamber position (knee raised, foot cocked — the Dollyo or Ap Chagi chamber) held for max time. Correct chamber form required — not just any one-leg stand.
`✓ Kick chamber position — correct TKD form is the test condition`

**TKD Link**
All kicks (chamber stability) · balance under pressure · Poomsae flow between moves

**How to Run**
1. All athletes simultaneously take chamber position
2. Coach starts timer. Count aloud at 10s, 20s, 30s (creates good competitive energy)
3. Athlete calls their time when foot touches — partner/coach notes it
4. Run non-dominant side too. Note both.
5. Frame as challenge, not test: "beat your last number" not "pass/fail"

**Log Format**

| Name | Date | Dom (s) | Non-Dom (s) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

> Retest every 4–6 weeks under same conditions (same time in session, same position, same footwear). This is your progress reference.

**Group Format**
Team totals: sum of all hold times = team score. Group tries to beat last session's total — cooperative motivation without individual pressure.

### C2 · Atem-Augen-Fokus
*Breathing + gaze — parasympathetic cool-down*

`Cool-Down` `6–12 min` `All ages` `Parasympathetic + Gaze`

**Neuro-Target**
Parasympathetic nervous system activation (via extended exhale) + gaze stabilization. Brings HR and arousal back to baseline while training focused fixation — the same visual discipline needed for pre-fight calm.

**Taekwondo Technique**
Low Juchum Seogi (horse stance) held throughout. Correct depth, correct spine, arms in resting guard position. Stance is trained — this is not passive rest.
`✓ Juchum Seogi — stance held and monitored`

> The breathing protocol (4-in / 6-out extended exhale) is non-TKD — no TKD technique achieves this specific parasympathetic trigger. Justified for cool-down neuro-function.

**How to Run**
1. Take Juchum Seogi — coach checks depth and alignment
2. Fix gaze on one point on the wall at eye level — maintain throughout
3. Breathe: 4 counts in through nose, 6 counts out through mouth
4. Kids: 6–8 breaths. Adults: 10–12 breaths.
5. Silent. Coach watches stance — correct any collapse quietly.

**Why It Works Neurologically**
Extended exhale activates the vagus nerve and shifts the autonomic nervous system toward parasympathetic dominance — reducing cortisol, lowering HR, and beginning the recovery window. Simultaneous gaze fixation consolidates the VOR improvements made during the session. Performing this in a held TKD stance means the session ends with correct proprioceptive feedback, not collapse-posture.

## 📊 Assessment System
*Low-effort tracking that feels like training · retest rhythm · what progress looks like*

### In-Game Individual Metrics

| Game | Metric |
|---|---|
| C1 Balance Hold | seconds, dominant + non-dominant — **primary metric** |
| W1 Farben-Chagi | errors per 10 cues |
| M1 Zahlen-Kombi | correct combos / attempts |
| M2 Vestibular Dollyo | stable / stumble / fall per 5 reps |
| M3 Einbein-Poomsae | hold time + arm-technique errors |
| M4 Perturbation | sequence errors vs. baseline clean run |

### Group / Team Formats

- **Relay Farben-Chagi:** team score = correct kicks per minute as relay chain
- **Team Balance Total:** sum of all C1 hold times = session team score. Post it. Compare weekly.
- **Kombi Tournament:** pairs compete — who completes a clean 4-combo sequence first, bracket format
- **Perturbation Grade:** group counts each other's sequence errors. Lowest error count wins round.

### Retest Rhythm

| Interval | What to retest |
|---|---|
| Every 4–6 weeks | C1 Balance Hold (same position, same session slot) |
| Every 6 weeks | W1 Farben-Chagi (errors/10 cues, 2 rounds) |
| Monthly | M4 Perturbation Poomsae (errors vs. clean baseline) |

### What "Better" Looks Like in TKD Terms

- **Faster first-movement after cue** in Farben-Chagi — visibly shorter lag before kick launches
- **No stumble-step** out of Vestibular Dollyo — landing absorbed cleanly into stance
- **Poomsae sequence without freeze-pause** on next move — especially holding up under Perturbation pressure
- **Steadier single-leg chamber** — kick held longer before execution, no hip wobble on chamber
- **Eyes stay on opponent during lateral footwork** — observable in sparring rounds, not just drills
- **Kids specifically:** less giggling/stumbling by rounds 4–6 of vestibular drills; more deliberate self-correction replacing pure reaction
- **Combination fluency:** Zahlen-Kombi sequences run without visible "internal search" between techniques
- **Balance hold time improvement:** +5–10s over 6 weeks is significant; +15s+ indicates strong vestibular/proprioceptive adaptation
